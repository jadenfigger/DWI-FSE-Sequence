# Static imaging phantom qualification

`examples/combined_factor_phantom.py` simulates a real two-shot imaging acquisition rather than applying echo amplitudes to an invented k-space image. Both shots are imaging shots: the selected protocol has `nav_on=0`, so shot indices `[0,1]` select views 0 and 8. Each shot acquires eight echoes with 128 complex ADC samples, giving all 16 distinct phase-encoding lines. Requested diffusion encoding is b=1000 s/mm² along read, TE is 36 ms, ESP is 16 ms, and TR is 2 s.

The object fills a 35-mm FOV with a 28 × 22-mm ellipse, a 6-mm circular insert with density 1.8, and a 1.2-mm disk with density 3.0. The smaller disk is below the 2.19-mm phase-direction sampling resolution and is included as a resolution challenge. Object mass is represented by equal-mass, scrambled Sobol points, with independent spatial coordinates and uniform support over z=±1 mm. All components have T1=1.5 s and T2=80 ms. Off-resonance is exactly zero and there is no T2-prime distribution. Finite-duration sinc RF and all played gradients are simulated. Spins remain stationary: playing diffusion gradients does **not** simulate diffusion attenuation or Brownian motion.

The reconstruction uses exact primary-pathway k-space coordinates at every acquired ADC sample and a direct nonuniform adjoint on a 128 × 16 grid. An independent piecewise-linear gradient integral resets at excitation centers and conjugates at refocusing centers; it agrees with PyPulseq's trajectory to less than 10⁻⁹ cycles/m. The acquired phase-encoding spacing is quantized by the scanner arithmetic and is retained rather than replacing it with an ideal Cartesian grid. There is no density compensation or data-derived phase or amplitude fit. The known prescribed zero-error RF/receiver phase is removed consistently; the imposed excitation phase error is not used to adapt reconstruction, so its echo-parity phase effects remain.

The analytic reference uses the exact Fourier transforms of the ellipse and circular inserts, the same acquired trajectory, uniform 2-mm slice support, instantaneous perfect RF, and T1/T2 evolution. The second shot's ideal initial magnetization is approximately 0.709 of equilibrium; the reference includes this saturation and the longitudinal inversions by all eight refocusing pulses. A separate encoding-only analytic reference sets one common scale for every displayed image and magnitude metric. No image receives its own gain normalization.

The instantaneous-RF reference is an encoding sanity check, **not** a ground-truth finite-RF image. Its slice profile differs from the finite sinc RF profile. Consequently, NRMSE against that reference includes slice-profile, relaxation-history, and other RF effects; it cannot be called an isolated artifact measure. Likewise, signal outside the object includes ordinary truncation ringing and sampling error, not just unwanted pathways.

The controls are the original sequence, centered increasing crushers `[1,1,1.4,1.8,2.2,2.6,3,3.4]`, and centered increasing crushers with alternating signs. Added finalists are a centered weak ramp `[1,1,1.2,1.4,1.6,1.8,2,2.2]` and the centered alternating high/low permutation `[1,-1,3.4,-1.4,3,-1.8,2.6,-2.2]`. All preserve the special first-refocusing baseline. Conditions are (RF scale, excitation phase error) = `(1,0°)`, `(.8,0°)`, `(.8,90°)`, and held-out `(.9,22.5°)`.

The audit compares the 10-µs RF step with 5 µs, repeats object sampling with another seed, compares 8192 with 32768 spins, and tests an on-slice-center, calibrated-RF acquisition at 1-µs RF steps against an independently evaluated discrete ideal primary. The operational image-ranking tolerance is at most 5% complex image NRMSE between seeds. An 8192-spin exploratory run failed decisively: seed changes produced approximately 54–56% complex image NRMSE, while RF step changes were below 0.05%. That preliminary audit is retained in `combined_phantom_audit_8192.json`. Increasing spatial sampling is therefore required before using phantom image metrics to rank candidates.

Final metrics and qualification status are in `combined_phantom_metrics.csv` and `combined_phantom_audit.json`. The figure is in `../figures/combined_factor_phantom.png`; large raw acquired signals, actual trajectories, reconstruction arrays, and configurations remain in `../../runs/combined_phantom/`. The final audit's `image_sampling_qualified` flag controls whether model-specific image ranking is supported. If false, the figure is an explicitly unqualified visualization and image-quality improvement remains unverified.

The completed 32768-spin audit still fails: complex image NRMSE between seeds is 27.39–28.56% across all five cases at RF scale .8, phase error 0°. Differences between 8192 and 32768 spins are 35.88–36.90%. The RF-step signal differences are only 0.0281–0.0289%, and the calibrated center-slice encoding check differs from the ideal discrete primary by 0.3846%. These separate checks show that trajectory/receiver handling and RF stepping are accurate enough for this experiment, while the full-volume spatial integration is not. The analytic-object ideal-primary image quadrature error is approximately 6.35% for original and 2.64% for centered cases in the zero-error condition; it also does not bound the error of the full finite-RF signal. Do not select a schedule based on the image peaks, leakage estimates, or instantaneous-reference NRMSE in this table.

Reproduce with `python examples/combined_factor_phantom.py --n 32768`. After a complete run, `--reuse` regenerates tables and plots from the cached acquired ADC signals and finished audit. Results are numerical static-phantom qualification only; scanner validation still requires measurements with realistic diffusion, motion, off-resonance, coils, and reconstruction.

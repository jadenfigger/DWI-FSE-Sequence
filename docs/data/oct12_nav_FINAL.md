# October 12 navigator analysis — final

This note closes the navigator-layout, per-shot correction, ghosting, and per-echo ADC work from the October 12 scanner session. Analysis code is in `oct12_navigators.py` and `oct12_nav_*.py`; machine-readable results are in `oct12_navigators.json`, `oct12_ghosting.csv`, and `oct12_navigator_echoes.csv`. Figures are in `docs/figures/physical_scanner_2026-10-12/`.

## Scan N: recovered layout and limits

Scan N is v1.83 with `nav_on=2`. Its MRD reserves 4 × 153 views, but only the first 4 × 119 views contain data: 7 volume-navigator echoes followed by 16 shots of 7 imaging echoes. The remaining 136 slots are zero. The stream period and the repeated volume-navigator starts confirm the 119-view volume length. The v1.83 PPL has `views_per_seg` reduced from 9 to 8 for `nav_on=2`; because its PPL comparisons return −1 for TRUE, `crusher_etl = views_per_seg + (nav_on==2)` becomes 7. The echo loop therefore stops before the eighth imaging echo or the per-shot navigator echo. R, acquired with v1.912, uses a separate nine-echo calculation and contains the expected volume and per-shot navigators.

For header-compatible reconstruction, each N volume is split at 119 views. Its first 7 samples are the volume navigator. The remaining 112 samples are placed at `pe_rows(128,8,1).reshape(16,8)[:,:7]`, with the absent eighth echo in every shot left zero-filled. This leaves ky rows 0–7 and 120–127 empty. The acquired ordering is exactly the same as `pe_rows(112,7,1)` after subtracting a common 8-row origin. A 112-row FFT is therefore a compact representation of those lines, while the sparse 128-row image retains the scanner grid and requires the correct pixel scaling. Neither reconstruction restores the 16 unacquired lines.

The ordinary `split_mode2` reconstruction is visibly scrambled and has volume-to-M correlations of 0.40, 0.40, 0.34, and 0.24. The recovered sparse reconstruction gives object correlations of 0.988, 0.987, 0.976, and 0.971 for b=10, 1000, 3000, and 6000 s/mm². Object NRMSE is 0.065, 0.082, 0.089, and 0.100. The b10 result is close to the independent Q-centre-vs-M repeat NRMSE (0.057); the b1000 and b3000 differences (0.117 and 0.071) are also on the scale of repeat variation and noise. Free row-scale fits across N volumes are 0.889–0.894, median 0.891.

There is a separate phase-encode geometry issue in N and R. Both store `gp_init_var=-1125` with `NO_VIEWS=153`; the v1.83/v1.912 PPL reduces its scale denominator to 136, while nav-on-1 scans use −1000 and 136. The resulting phase-encode step is 1.125× the nav-on-1 step. The inferred phase FOV is 31.1 mm (rather than 35 mm), or 0.243 mm per 128-row pixel (rather than 0.273 mm). R-to-Q free row-scale fits are 0.877–0.902 across its three slices and two low-b volumes, median about 0.892, consistent with the predicted 0.889. Treat ADC and ghost comparisons across N/R and nav-on-1 scans with this scale difference in mind.

The corrected N magnitude and complex k-space, navigator train, b-values, directions, acquired-row list, zero-filled-row list, and reconstruction metadata are packaged in [`oct12_N_recovered.npz`](oct12_N_recovered.npz). The preview is [`nav_N_fixed_vs_M.png`](../figures/physical_scanner_2026-10-12/nav_N_fixed_vs_M.png). N has no per-shot navigator, so no per-shot phase correction can be estimated for it.

## Per-shot navigator benefit in R

The R test compares uncorrected images with the shipped constant-plus-linear phase correction and a constant-only variant. The primary measure is normalized excess phase-encode background over the readout control background; lower is better. At the centre slice, the plain-to-shipped mean metric changes from 0.0116 to 0.0127 at b1000 (slightly worse), and from 0.0118 to 0.0100 at b3000 (slightly better). The constant-only variant gives 0.0056 at b1000 and 0.0081 at b3000. Per-volume outcomes vary: for the three b1000 volumes, constant-only improves two and is nearly tied on one; at b3000, it improves two and worsens one. At off-centre slices, the shipped correction worsens the mean b1000 metric from 0.0178 to 0.0416; the SNR≥8 gate suppresses those off-centre corrections.

Navigator SNR is high at b10 (median 66.7), moderate at b1000 (15–18), and lower at b3000 (6.3–8.2). Even after excluding the reference shot, the fitted readout phase-ramp spread is about 13–27° across the FOV, with a consistent even/odd-shot component. The data show that per-shot phase varies, but the current experiment does not establish an overall benefit from the shipped correction. Constant-only looks promising in this small set; it needs repeated scans and an independently validated motion/ghost measure before being selected.

## Ghosting

The ghost measure is `(mean magnitude in PE background − mean magnitude in RO control) / mean magnitude in the object`. Background strips lie outside the dilated object; the PE strip spans the object in readout, and the RO strip is the control. Reliable rows require object SNR≥4 and at least 150 object pixels. For ex-vivo b≤1000 the zones exclude the whole b10 tube; at b≥3000 they exclude only the current brain mask, so the PBS-filled tube interior contributes to the high-b background measure.

On the centre slice, ex-vivo v1.83 scans have larger mean excess than the v1.912 scans in these comparisons: b1000 +x, 0.0251 vs 0.0137; b1000 +z, 0.0230 vs 0.0050; b3000 +x, 0.0485 vs 0.0108; b3000 +y, 0.0322 vs 0.0133; b3000 +z, 0.0383 vs 0.0089; and b6000 +z, 0.0266 vs 0.0019. These are descriptive associations with sequence version, not an isolated version effect: the scan set is small, some comparisons are unpaired, and N/R have the 31.1-mm PE FOV. In vivo centre-slice values vary by direction and scan; iA b3000 +y is high (0.144), while corresponding iB is below the reliability threshold. Use the CSV for individual slices and uncertainty estimates.

## Per-echo and k-space ADC

Navigator amplitude for echo *n* is `sqrt(max(sum |nav_n|² − samples × noise_power, 0))`; complex-noise power is estimated from the median outer k-space corners. Nominal ADC is calculated per echo as `ln(A_b10,n / A_b,n) / Δb`; the effective-b version uses the modelled b-trace difference. The k-band estimate independently sums noise-subtracted `|k|²` over imaging lines acquired at each echo number, then forms the same per-echo b10-to-high-b ratio. Ex-vivo brain and PBS projections are also separated by column masks and a two-component complex fit. Reported echo trends compare echo 1 with the weighted mean of echoes 2…ETL; k-band high-b rows are retained only when both amplitudes exceed twice their estimated standard error.

The navigator-projection ADC and k-band ADC do not show the same echo trend. In vivo iA, total navigator ADC is nearly flat from echo 1 to later echoes (about −2% to −1% at b6000 across x/y/z), while k-band ADC is lower by about 34–42% over later usable echoes. Ex-vivo b6000 +z gives a +26.5% later-vs-first change in total navigator ADC for M (v1.83) and +3.7% for O (v1.912), while the k-band estimate changes by −4.3% for M (5 usable echoes) and −14.9% for O (4 usable echoes). At b3000, v1.83 Q total and brain-only navigator ADC rise roughly 15–27% over the train, while its k-band estimate is close to flat; v1.912 R total ADC is nearly flat, but k-band ADC falls about 13–19% for the three directions. These shifts point to echo/k-space dependence in the acquired signal or its noise floor; they are not evidence that tissue ADC itself changes with echo number.

The high-b k-band estimates lose usable echoes as signal approaches the noise floor, especially for the ex-vivo scans. Nominal b-values also omit the measured diffusion-waveform deviations captured by the effective-b model. The volume navigator is an unencoded readout projection rather than an image, and the k-band signal mixes brain, PBS, and background. Treat per-echo differences as acquisition diagnostics, not tissue-property estimates.
